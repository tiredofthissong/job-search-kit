# LinkedIn section guidance

LinkedIn's own help center blocked automated reading when this kit was researched (Nov 2026), so this draws on LinkedIn's official blog and recruiter pages plus university career offices: Purdue Global (Nov 10, 2026) https://www.purdueglobal.edu/blog/careers/linkedin-guide-job-seekers/ ; Penn West (Dec 3, 2025) https://career.pennwest.edu/blog/2025/12/03/should-you-let-recruiters-know-youre-open-to-work-on-linkedin/ ; LinkedIn Recruiter overview https://business.linkedin.com/hire/resources/talent-acquisition/how-to-use-linkedin-recruiter ; LinkedIn blog on Open to Work (2020) https://blog.linkedin.com/2020/october/29/the-benefits-of-sharing-with-your-community-you-re-open-to-work . Some LinkedIn statistics are from 2017 to 2020.

## How recruiters find people
Recruiters search by job title, skills, keywords and location. So his headline, current or target titles, skills and location should match the jobs he wants.

## Headline (up to 220 characters)
- Lead with the target role, then 2 or 3 strengths or tools, then a proof point.
- Example: "Customer Service Representative | De-escalation, Point of Sale, Microsoft Office | 4 years helping 100+ customers a day"
- Avoid jokes or vague titles ("Ninja", "Seeking opportunities").

## About (2 to 4 short paragraphs)
1. Who he is and what he's looking for, in plain first person.
2. Two specific wins with numbers from the Story Bank.
3. How he works with people (patient, calm, learns systems fast) with one example.
4. One line on availability (remote / hybrid / local area) and a friendly close.

## Experience
- Same titles, companies and dates as the resume.
- 2 to 4 short bullets per job, reused from the resume.

## Skills
- List at least 5, chosen from words in real postings he wants (customer service, troubleshooting, ticketing systems, Microsoft Office, de-escalation). Only skills he really has.
- Pin the 2 most relevant to the top.

## Photo and location
- Current, clear photo of just him. Location set to his city (recruiters filter by it even for remote roles).

## Open to Work
- Two settings: **Recruiters only** (visible to recruiters using LinkedIn's paid tools; LinkedIn tries to hide it from recruiters at his current company) or **All LinkedIn members** (adds the green #OpenToWork frame).
- Suggest **All members** if he's not working now, **Recruiters only** if he is employed.
- It lasts a few months; LinkedIn emails before it ends.

## Referrals
- On postings he likes, check whether he knows anyone at the company (LinkedIn shows connections). A referral is one of the strongest ways to get an interview. The job-tailor skill can draft a short, polite referral ask for him to send.
