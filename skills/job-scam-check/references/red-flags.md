# Job scam red flags

Sources: FTC "Job Scams" (modified Aug 13, 2026) https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams ; FTC alert on job texts (Apr 2026) https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/04/job-offer-text-probably-scam ; FTC task scams (Aug 2025) https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/08/how-spot-and-avoid-task-scams ; FTC work-from-home scams (Jun 2025) https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/06/how-avoid-work-home-job-scams ; FBI IC3 public service announcements https://ic3.gov/PSA/2022/PSA220201 and https://ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240604 and https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2020/PSA200121 ; Indeed safe job search guidelines https://www.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/216354123-guidelines-for-a-safe-job-search

## Red (stop)
- Asks him to **pay** for anything to get or start the job: training, starter kit, certificate, background check fee, placement fee, equipment.
- Sends a **check** (for equipment or "supplies") and asks him to send some money back, buy gift cards, or pay a vendor.
- "Tasks", "optimization", "product boosting" or "rating" work that pays small amounts first, then asks for a deposit (often crypto) to unlock earnings.
- Reshipping packages from home.
- Asks for his Social Security number, bank account, ID photos or card number **before** a verified written offer.
- Asks him to buy a laptop or headset from "our vendor" with his own money to be repaid later.
- Recruits or interviews **only** by text, WhatsApp, Telegram or Signal. Per the FTC, real employers don't contact people that way out of the blue.

## Yellow (verify first)
- Offer with no real interview, or a same-day offer.
- Recruiter writes from a free email (gmail, yahoo, outlook) or a look-alike domain.
- The job isn't listed on the company's own careers page.
- Very high pay for simple work, vague duties, "no experience needed, start today".
- Pressure to decide fast or keep it secret.
- Company name copied from a real company but different contact details.

## How to verify
1. Find the company's official website by searching its name (not by clicking links in the message), open its Careers page, and look for the same job.
2. Compare the recruiter's email domain to the company's website domain.
3. Search the company name plus "scam" or "reviews".
4. Talk it over with someone he trusts.

## Normal after a real offer
I-9 employment eligibility form, a background check run by the employer, a direct-deposit form, an employer-shipped work computer, or a clear "use your own Windows PC" requirement listed in the posting.

## If you already paid or shared details
1. Contact your bank or payment app right away and say it's fraud; ask to stop or reverse the payment.
2. If you shared your Social Security number, go to IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan.
3. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online.
4. Stop replying to the scammer.

## Where to report
- ReportFraud.ftc.gov
- ic3.gov (FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center)
- The job site where it was posted (use its "report job" button).
